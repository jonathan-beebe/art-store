# Carrier Tracking APIs — Cost & Access Requirements

**Research date:** August 21, 2026
**Scope:** USPS, UPS, FedEx — retrieving package tracking information via API
**Trello card:** https://trello.com/c/xxHVqEef

---

## Bottom line

UPS and FedEx tracking APIs are free. USPS is **not** free unless you created the label yourself.

| Carrier | Free? | Hard requirement | Practical blocker |
|---|---|---|---|
| USPS | Only if the Mailer ID in the barcode is yours | Business Portal account, OAuth2 app, CRID + MID | Paid license required for third-party tracking |
| UPS | Yes | ups.com profile + UPS account number, OAuth2 app | Redistribution restrictions in the Technology Agreement |
| FedEx | Yes | Developer portal org + project; account number for production key | Push notifications are a paid add-on |

**The decision hinges on one question:** are you tracking labels you generated, or labels generated by someone else? If the latter, USPS moves from free to a licensed commercial relationship and the economics change.

---

## USPS

### Cost
Access changed materially on **April 1, 2026** under the Tracking API Access Control initiative. USPS now gates tracking by who owns the Mailer ID (MID) embedded in the package barcode.

- **Free — Shippers.** You ship direct to customers, create your own label, and the MID in the barcode is registered to you. Continued no-cost access via Tracking API, Tracking Webhook, or Scan Event Extract.
- **Free — Platforms and consolidators.** Label providers and consolidators keep no-cost access, but only when authorized by the shipper to use one of their active Merchant Access Tokens through the Business Portal platform authorization process.
- **Paid — Service providers and others.** Anyone providing reporting, analytics, or similar services to a shipper pays. Access is subject to a monthly fee, a signed Intellectual Property agreement, and an Enterprise Payment System (EPS) account. You must also be authorized to access the specific MID.

*Confidence: HIGH.* This comes directly from the USPS API Access page.

### Pricing
USPS does **not** publish paid-tier pricing. A widely circulated figure of $599/month originates from a single developer's blog post describing an unexecuted quote (Order Form 2026–0219) he received but did not sign. He also notes that USPS's license terms bind executed licensees to confidentiality on pricing.

*Confidence: LOW.* Treat $599 as one data point from one quote, not a published rate. If USPS cost matters to the decision, submit an inquiry through the USPS Email Us form and get a quote in writing.

### What's required
1. USPS Business Portal account at cop.usps.com
2. Developer Portal app → yields Consumer Key and Consumer Secret
3. Valid Customer Registration ID (CRID) and Mailer ID (MID) — obtained from your Business Customer Gateway administrator
4. OAuth 2.0 bearer token from `https://apis.usps.com/oauth2/v3/token`
5. Review and accept the updated Terms & Conditions under My Apps → Developer Apps → Manage
6. For paid access: EPS account, signed Order Form and IP Agreement, Business Portal permission configuration

### Migration warning
The legacy **USPS Web Tools platform was retired January 25, 2026.** Any inherited integration pointing at `shippingapis.com` is already dead or degrading. Web Tools tracking specifically ended per a March 5, 2026 industry alert. New work goes to `apis.usps.com`.

### Notable license terms
The developer license reportedly includes a unilateral modification clause (USPS can change terms by posting updates, with continued use constituting acceptance) and a no-competing-services clause broad enough to raise questions for any app that displays tracking events. *Confidence: MEDIUM — sourced from the same blog analysis, not from a license document I reviewed directly.* Read the actual agreement before signing.

## UPS

### Cost
**Free.** The UPS Developer Kit knowledge base states the Tracking API is free to license with no hidden fees or charges.

*Confidence: HIGH on the cost claim.*

### What's required
1. A ups.com profile and a UPS account number
2. An app created in the UPS Developer Portal → yields Client ID and Client Secret
3. OAuth 2.0 client credentials flow against `https://onlinetools.ups.com/security/v1/oauth/token` (test: `wwwcie.ups.com`)
4. Agreement to the UPS Technology Agreement

### Limits
No documented rate limits. The OpenAPI specification on UPS's GitHub does not mention them. There is a "Blocked Merchant" 403 response in the spec, suggesting account-level enforcement against suspected abuse, but no published volume threshold.

*Confidence: MEDIUM.* "Undocumented" is not the same as "unlimited." Build in backoff handling.

### Legal constraints that matter for product design
The UPS Technology Agreement restricts your application from distributing information received through the APIs to third parties other than persons with a bona fide interest (shipper, receiver, third-party payer, or authorized service providers). It also imposes requirements on displaying the UPS mark and copyright notices, and prohibits altering returned tracking data.

If the product surfaces UPS tracking to anyone outside that list, get legal review.

### Migration note
UPS completed migration from legacy XML APIs with access keys to OAuth 2.0 REST APIs in 2024. Build against REST only.

## FedEx

### Cost
**Free.** FedEx states plainly that it does not charge a fee to use its APIs, documentation, or sample code, subject to terms of use.

*Confidence: HIGH.*

### Limits (Track API / Basic Integrated Visibility)
- **100,000 API calls per day** per capability per project
- **1,400 transactions per 10 seconds** per project (exceeding returns HTTP 429 and restricts transactions for the remainder of the window)
- **Up to 30 tracking numbers per request** via batch tracking
- Tracking data available for **90 days after delivery** for FedEx Express, Ground, and Custom Critical
- New package data can take up to 24 hours to appear

For context: FedEx gives away roughly 30× the daily volume that USPS now charges for.

### What's required
1. A FedEx user ID and account
2. Create an **organization** in the FedEx Developer Portal (this is the container for users, shipping accounts, and projects)
3. Create a project and select the Track API
4. Accept the Developer Portal License Agreement (DPLA); an End User License Agreement is executed per shipping account added
5. Test key: generated immediately, **no shipping account required**
6. Production key: **requires a valid FedEx account number**
7. OAuth 2.0 client credentials against `https://apis.fedex.com/oauth/token` (sandbox: `apis-sandbox.fedex.com`); tokens valid ~3,600 seconds

**Certification:** Track does not require label certification. That requirement applies to the Ship API.

### The paid tier
**Advanced Integrated Visibility / Shipment Visibility Webhook** — near-real-time push updates for US-based accounts and tracking numbers, including estimated delivery windows, picture proof of delivery, and GPS coordinates. Subscribers pay a monthly fee based on the count of tracking numbers, with a 30-day free trial followed by a 12-month initial term (cancellable anytime through the portal). Requires a US FedEx account number for billing.

If polling at 100K/day is sufficient, you never touch this tier.

## Open questions to resolve before building

1. **Whose labels are we tracking?** This single question determines whether USPS costs nothing or requires a commercial license. Everything else is downstream of it.
2. **What's our polling volume?** An app polling every 5 minutes generates ~288 requests per day per label. At scale this matters for FedEx's 100K/day cap and for any USPS per-request pricing.
3. **Do we redistribute tracking data?** Both UPS and USPS have contractual language that constrains showing tracking data to parties without a direct interest in the shipment.
4. **Build vs. aggregate.** Three separate OAuth integrations plus a possible USPS license versus a single multi-carrier aggregator. EasyPost and Shippo are commonly cited at roughly $0.02 per label tracked; AfterShip and 17TRACK are alternatives. These figures are secondhand — verify current pricing directly before comparing.
