# art-store vs Etsy — Seller Cost Analysis

**Compiled:** 2026-08-29
**Source for all Etsy rates:** `etsy-fees-and-pricing-model.md` in this folder (Etsy-owned pages, Feb 2026 policy).
**Source for art-store's model:** `business-model-analysis.md` — flat all-in fee, platform absorbs payment processing.
**Modelled at art-store's recommended 8%** unless stated. All figures US seller, USD listing, no sales tax, no FX.

**Validation:** every Etsy figure in this document reproduces the worked examples in §5.1 and §5.2 of the research file exactly ($10 → 14.0%, $50 → 10.4%, $100 → 9.95%, $50 with 15% ads → 25.4%). The model is not inventing Etsy's numbers.

---

## Bottom line up front

**1. art-store at 8% beats Etsy's real cost for almost every seller — but not against Etsy's best case.**
Etsy's advertised 6.5% is not what sellers pay. The true baseline is **10.4%** on a $50 order and **11–15%** annually once listing carry and ads attribution are included. art-store's flat 8% beats that everywhere. **It does not beat an Etsy seller using Share & Save**, who pays as little as **7.2%**.

**2. The Share & Save problem is your single biggest competitive exposure, and it targets your best sellers.**
Etsy refunds 4% to sellers who bring their own traffic. That drops a professional seller to **7.4%** — cheaper than art-store's 8%. **The sellers most able to exploit it are exactly the ones you most want: established artists with their own audience.** At 8% you lose the pitch to them. See §4.

**3. Your quiet advantage is the listing fee, and nobody is talking about it.**
Etsy charges $0.20 per listing per 4 months whether or not it sells — **$0.60/year per SKU of pure carrying cost.** art-store charges nothing. For a catalogue-heavy, low-velocity artist — 500 works listed, $2,000 of annual sales — Etsy's listing fees alone are **15% of GMV before a single other fee.** That seller pays **25.4% to Etsy and 8% to you** — and 59% of the Etsy bill is charged on work that never sold. This is the most winnable segment in the market and it is invisible in every headline fee comparison.

**4. The $10,000 Offsite Ads cliff is your strongest narrative, but it is a story, not a saving.**
Once an Etsy shop crosses $10,000 in trailing-365-day sales it is enrolled in Offsite Ads **for the lifetime of the shop, with no opt-out.** That is a permanent, uncapped-by-choice 12% on attributed orders. art-store has no equivalent. It is the most emotionally resonant thing you can say to a growing seller — and it only bites sellers who have already succeeded.

**5. The fee saving is only motivating for sellers you will struggle to move.**
A hobbyist at $1,320/year saves **$56** by switching. A full-time seller at $60,000/year saves **$1,920**. The saving scales with seller size — and so does the cost of leaving Etsy's reviews, search ranking and buyer traffic behind. **Your pitch is strongest on the sellers with the most to lose by moving.** That tension is the central strategic problem, not the fee rate.

**6. None of this matters if art-store does not bring buyers.**
Etsy's fees buy demand. A seller paying 10.4% to a platform that delivers orders is getting better value than one paying 8% to a platform that delivers none. **Every number in this document is a cost comparison, not a value comparison**, and cost is the weaker half of the argument. See §8.

## 1. The two fee structures side by side

| | **Etsy** (US seller) | **art-store** |
|---|---|---|
| Headline rate | 6.5% transaction | **8% flat, all-in** |
| Payment processing | 3% + $0.25, paid by seller | **Included — platform absorbs it** |
| Listing fee | $0.20 per listing per 4 months, **and** $0.20 per unit sold | **None** |
| Offsite Ads | 15% (<$10k) / 12% (≥$10k) on attributed orders. **Mandatory for life once past $10k** | **None** |
| On-platform ads | Optional CPC, seller-set budget | Not modelled |
| Currency conversion | 2.5% if listing currency ≠ payout currency | Not modelled |
| Regulatory operating fee | 0.05%–1.97% (non-US sellers) | **None** |
| Subscriptions | Etsy Plus $10/mo, Pattern $15/mo | **None** |
| Traffic rebate | **Share & Save: −4%** for self-driven traffic | **None — this is the gap** |
| Shop set-up | ~$15 one-time (MEDIUM confidence) | Not modelled |
| **Number of fee lines a seller must reason about** | **9+** | **1** |

**The structural difference is not the rate. It is the count.** An Etsy seller cannot answer "what does this cost me?" without knowing their AOV, their catalogue size, their sell-through, their ads attribution rate and their trailing-twelve-month revenue. **An art-store seller multiplies by 0.08.** That legibility is worth more than it looks and is the easiest thing to market.

## 2. Cost per order, by order value

What a seller pays on a single order. Etsy's fixed components ($0.20 listing + $0.25 processing = **$0.45 per order**) do not scale down; art-store has no fixed component because the platform absorbs processing.

| Order value | Etsy baseline | Etsy + 20% ads attribution | Etsy + Share & Save | **art-store @ 8%** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $10 | 14.0% | 17.0% | 10.0% | **8.0%** |
| $25 | 11.3% | 14.3% | 7.3% | **8.0%** |
| $50 | 10.4% | 13.4% | 6.4% | **8.0%** |
| $100 | 10.0% | 13.0% | 5.9% | **8.0%** |
| $250 | 9.7% | 12.7% | 5.7% | **8.0%** |

**Read the $10 row.** Etsy's baseline is **14.0%** — the $0.45 of fixed fees is 4.5 points on its own. art-store charges 8% flat. **The lower the order value, the wider your advantage**, which is the opposite of how most fee comparisons behave and is worth saying out loud to sellers of prints, stickers, cards and small originals.

**Read the $250 row.** Etsy converges to 9.7% and Share & Save gets to 5.7%. **At high order values your advantage narrows and then inverts.**

## 3. Annual cost by seller archetype

Assumes a $50 AOV. "Carry" is the listing fee on unsold inventory — $0.20 per listing per 4-month term, three terms a year.

| Seller | Annual GMV | Listings | Etsy baseline | Etsy @20% attribution | Etsy + Share & Save | **art-store @ 8%** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hobbyist | $1,320 | 40 | $161 (12.2%) | $201 (15.2%) | $108 (8.2%) | **$106** (8.0%) |
| Side business | $6,000 | 150 | $714 (11.9%) | $894 (14.9%) | $474 (7.9%) | **$480** (8.0%) |
| Professional | $24,000 | 400 | $2,736 (11.4%) | $3,312 (13.8%) | $1,776 (7.4%) | **$1,920** (8.0%) |
| Full-time | $60,000 | 800 | $6,720 (11.2%) | $8,160 (13.6%) | $4,320 (7.2%) | **$4,800** (8.0%) |

### 3a. What the seller saves by switching

| Seller | vs Etsy baseline | vs Etsy @20% attribution | vs Etsy + Share & Save |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hobbyist | **$56** | **$95** | $3 |
| Side business | **$234** | **$414** | ($6) |
| Professional | **$816** | **$1,392** | ($144) |
| Full-time | **$1,920** | **$3,360** | ($480) |

**Two things to take from this table.**

**The saving is real but small at the bottom.** $56/year does not move a hobbyist off a platform where their reviews and search history live. **Below roughly $5,000 of annual GMV, the fee argument is not an acquisition argument** — you would be recruiting those sellers on something else entirely.

**The Share & Save column is negative for every seller above hobbyist level.** An Etsy seller who drives their own traffic is already cheaper than you. See next section.

## 4. The Share & Save problem

This is the finding that works against the pitch, so it gets its own section.

Etsy refunds **4% of the transaction total** when a buyer arrives through the seller's own Share & Save link — their social, newsletter, or website — and buys within 30 days. It also **blocks the Offsite Ads fee** on that order. Effective Etsy transaction fee drops from 6.5% to **2.5%**.

### 4a. Where art-store's flat fee stops winning

The AOV at which Etsy + Share & Save becomes cheaper than art-store:

| art-store fee | vs Etsy baseline | vs Etsy + Share & Save |
|---|---|---|
| **6%** | Never — art-store always cheaper | **Etsy cheaper above $90 AOV** |
| **7%** | Never — art-store always cheaper | **Etsy cheaper above $30 AOV** |
| **8%** | Never — art-store always cheaper | **Etsy cheaper above $18 AOV** |
| **9%** | Never — art-store always cheaper | **Etsy cheaper above $13 AOV** |

**At 8%, any seller with an AOV above $18 who drives their own traffic pays less on Etsy than on art-store.** At 6% that threshold rises to $90 — but §9 of the business model rules out 6% on your own economics, so that escape route is closed.

### 4b. Why this matters more than the arithmetic suggests

The sellers who can use Share & Save effectively are the ones with an audience — an Instagram following, a mailing list, a personal site. **Those are precisely the sellers a new marketplace most needs**, because they arrive carrying their own demand and do not depend on you for traffic you cannot yet supply.

**Etsy has priced its rate card to retain exactly the sellers you must win.** That is not an accident; §7.5 of the research file reads Share & Save as Etsy pricing its own demand generation. A seller who brings their own buyers pays Etsy 2.5% for infrastructure. That is a genuinely good deal and you cannot beat it at 8% flat.

### 4c. Three responses, none free

- **Match it.** An art-store equivalent — a lower rate on seller-driven traffic — costs you real margin. At 8% dropping to 4% on self-driven orders, your net take after processing and 1% losses falls from 3.5% to **−0.5%**. **You cannot afford a 4-point rebate.** A 1–2 point rebate is affordable and less compelling.
- **Ignore it and compete on the fee stack's other nine lines.** Legibility, no listing fees, no ads cliff. Strong for catalogue-heavy and low-AOV sellers; weak for the audience-owning seller you want.
- **Compete on something that is not price.** Better payout terms, better seller tooling, curation, a buyer audience they cannot reach on Etsy. This is the only durable answer and it is not a fee decision.

**My read:** do not try to beat Share & Save on rate. You will lose that trade and damage your own model doing it.

## 5. The listing fee — your quiet structural advantage

Etsy charges **$0.20 per listing per 4-month term**, whether the item sells or not, plus $0.20 per unit sold on multi-quantity listings. Annualised, that is **$0.60 per SKU per year of pure carrying cost**. art-store charges nothing per listing.

This is invisible in per-order comparisons and brutal for artists with deep back catalogues and low velocity — a very common shape in original art.

### 5a. Catalogue carrying cost as a share of GMV

Etsy listing carry only, before any other fee, at a $50 AOV:

| Listings | Annual carry | As % of $2,000 GMV | As % of $6,000 GMV | As % of $24,000 GMV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | $60.00 | **3.0%** | 1.0% | 0.2% |
| 250 | $150.00 | **7.5%** | 2.5% | 0.6% |
| 500 | $300.00 | **15.0%** | 5.0% | 1.2% |
| 1000 | $600.00 | **30.0%** | 10.0% | 2.5% |

### 5b. The worked case — the catalogue-heavy artist

**500 works listed, $2,000 of annual sales, $50 AOV.** A painter with a decade of inventory online and modest turnover.

| Line | Etsy | art-store @ 8% |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction fee | $130.00 | — |
| Payment processing | $70.00 | **Included** |
| Listing fee on units sold | $8.00 | — |
| **Listing carry on unsold inventory** | **$300.00** | **$0.00** |
| Flat fee | — | $160.00 |
| **Total** | **$508.00** | **$160.00** |
| **As % of GMV** | **25.4%** | **8.0%** |

With 20% ads attribution that Etsy total rises to **$568.00 — 28.4% of GMV.**

**$300.00 of that bill — 59% of everything they pay Etsy — is charged on work that did not sell.** This seller pays Etsy **3.2×** what they would pay you, and most of the difference is rent on unsold inventory.

**This is your best acquisition segment and I would build the pitch around it.** It is quantifiable, it is emotionally infuriating to the seller, and no headline fee comparison surfaces it. It also has a product implication: **unlimited free listings must be a permanent commitment, not an introductory offer** — the moment you charge per listing you become Etsy with fewer buyers.

## 6. The $10,000 Offsite Ads cliff

From the research file, HIGH confidence: shops under $10,000 in trailing-365-day sales may opt out of Offsite Ads. **Once a shop crosses $10,000, participation is mandatory "for the lifetime of your shop"** at 12% of attributed order value, capped at $100 per order. The opt-out is permanently revoked.

### 6a. What crossing the cliff costs

Annual Offsite Ads fees for a shop past $10,000, by attribution rate:

| Annual GMV | 10% attributed | 20% attributed | 30% attributed |
|---|---|---|---|
| $12,000 | $144 | $288 | $432 |
| $24,000 | $288 | $576 | $864 |
| $60,000 | $720 | $1,440 | $2,160 |
| $120,000 | $1,440 | $2,880 | $4,320 |

**On art-store all four columns are $0.** There is no ads fee, no threshold, and no irreversible enrolment.

### 6b. How to use this, and how not to

**The narrative is strong.** "Etsy's most successful sellers are permanently enrolled in a fee they cannot leave" is true, verifiable on Etsy's own help pages, and lands hard with anyone approaching $10,000.

**The honest caveat: nobody knows the attribution rate.** Etsy does not publish what share of orders get attributed to Offsite Ads, and the research file names this as the single biggest gap in the whole fee model — it swings the effective rate by more than 10 points. **The columns above are scenarios, not measurements.** If real attribution is 5%, the cliff costs a $24,000 seller $144/year and the outrage is disproportionate to the money.

**Use the irreversibility, not the number.** The permanence is the story; the amount is unverified.

## 8. The honest core — what Etsy's fees actually buy

Every number in this document compares **cost**. Cost is the weaker half of the argument, and I would rather say so than let the tables imply more than they support.

**An Etsy seller paying 11% is buying access to a buyer base.** An art-store seller paying 8% on a platform with no buyers is paying less for nothing. **The correct comparison is not 8% versus 11% — it is 8% of a smaller number versus 11% of a larger one.**

A seller doing $24,000/year on Etsy pays $2,736 and keeps $21,264. The same seller on art-store pays $1,920 — but only if they still sell $24,000. **If art-store delivers half the sales volume, they earn $11,040 and the 3-point fee saving is irrelevant.**

Run that properly. That seller keeps **$21,264** after Etsy's fees. To keep the same amount on art-store at 8% they need **$23,113 of sales — 96.3% of their Etsy volume.** In other words: **the entire fee advantage is wiped out by a 3.7% drop in sales.** A seller who loses one order in twenty-seven by moving is already worse off.

**That is the number to internalise.** A 3-point fee saving sounds material and is almost nothing next to volume risk. Fee competition buys you a conversation; it cannot survive a platform that sells less.

**This is the real product risk and it is the same conclusion the business model reached from the platform side.** Your 10,000-seller critical-mass thesis is fundamentally a bet that sellers attract buyers. The fee advantage buys you a hearing. It does not close the deal, and it will not retain anyone whose sales fall when they move.

## 9. Positioning implications

1. **Do not lead with the fee rate.** "8% versus Etsy's 6.5%" loses on the headline and requires a paragraph to unpick. **Lead with "one fee, no listing charges, no mandatory ads"** — the structure, not the number.

2. **Publish the comparison honestly, including Share & Save.** Sellers who know Etsy will find it themselves; being the one who raised it buys more credibility than the 4% costs you. Volunteering your weakness is a stronger position than being caught omitting it.

3. **Make unlimited free listings a permanent, explicit commitment.** It is your clearest structural advantage (§5), your best-differentiated segment, and a promise you can keep because listings cost you almost nothing.

4. **Build a comparison calculator, not a comparison table.** The Etsy stack is illegible enough that sellers cannot compute their own number. A calculator that takes AOV, listing count and annual sales and returns both bills is a better acquisition asset than any claim — and the arithmetic already exists in this document.

5. **Target catalogue-heavy, low-AOV, sub-$10k sellers first.** Largest relative saving, lowest switching cost, least dependent on the buyer traffic you do not yet have.

6. **Do not build a Share & Save clone.** A 4-point rebate takes your net take to −0.5%. If you must respond, 1–2 points is the affordable ceiling and it will not be decisive.

## 10. Open questions

- [ ] **Etsy's Offsite Ads attribution rate.** Unpublished, swings the comparison by 10+ points, and §6 is built on scenarios because of it. Highest-value gap.
- [ ] **What share of target sellers actually use Share & Save.** Determines whether §4 is a footnote or the main event.
- [ ] **art-store's real AOV and catalogue-size distribution.** §2 and §5 are the two places the advantage is largest; both depend on numbers you do not yet have.
- [ ] **Switching cost in seller terms** — reviews, ranking, saved-shop followers. Not a fee, and probably larger than every fee in this document.
- [ ] **Whether art-store charges sellers anything else** — payout fees, subscriptions, promoted placement. Every addition erodes the one-line-fee advantage that §1 identifies as the real differentiator.
- [ ] **Shipping labels.** Etsy marks up carrier rates (an undisclosed component of its 25.9% take rate). If art-store does not offer labels, sellers keep that cost elsewhere — this comparison ignores it entirely.
- [ ] **Non-US seller economics.** Flagged as a win in §7 but not modelled; the research file has the rates.
